Two-lane context engine · Evaluation · Experiment 3, absorbing E2

E3 Store Design Plan

Which way of storing what the batch lane learns gives an agent the most correct context per token, on our kind of data, and does the answer depend on which agent builds it and which agent reads it? This experiment decides what the context engine's store will be. It replaces E2 and E3 as separately specified with one staged comparison of file-based, graph-based, and retrieval designs, judged by the same 600 derived-gold probes.

Supersedes E2 plan v3, spec §7 E2 and E3 Claims C2 fidelity, C3 organisation Corpus Kafka R‑H1, 2026‑01 → 06 Headline agent‑curated store vs templated files and vs vector RAG at 8 k Status plan, nothing registered Prepared 2026‑09‑08

Summary

The experiment is a tournament with rules fixed in advance. Every store is built from the identical frozen stream, snapshotted after every fold, and read by the same tool-using reader under the same token budget. A frozen set of probes with gold derived from the JIRA changelog, PR metadata, and regex joins grades each store on five families: extraction, temporal, knowledge update, multi-source, and abstention. The stores are compared paired per probe, clustered by entity, with search cost and build cost beside every accuracy.

It runs in stages so that money is spent on what earlier stages showed to matter. Stage A builds the yardstick and passes the human gate. Stage B runs the stores that cost nothing to build. Stage C sweeps eleven store structures with one builder. Stage D takes the top structures and rebuilds them with other harnesses and models to test the interaction you asked about. Stage E varies the reader on the top stores. Stage F confirms the winner on a second, sealed probe set with three seeds. The winner's-curse control is that second probe set: no store is declared best on the probes it was selected with.

What comes out the other end

A ranked table of store designs on a Pareto of accuracy, search cost, and build cost, with confidence intervals; a statement of whether the ranking is stable across builders and readers; and a registered mapping from outcome to implementation change in harnext. The store we currently ship, F5, enters as one condition among seventeen with no privilege beyond being named in the primaries.

1 · The question

Two claims from the spec merge here. C2 says the batch lane keeps per-entity state that is correct and current with respect to facts re-derivable from the log. C3 says organising that state yields more correct context per token than unorganised alternatives and does not erode over weeks of stream. Testing C2 alone needed one store; testing C3 needs many, and every C2 metric is computed by the C3 machinery anyway. The merged question is:

Question

Given an identical heterogeneous event stream, which store design lets a fixed reader answer derived-gold questions most correctly per token read, at what build cost, and is that ordering stable when the builder agent, the builder model, and the reader agent change?

Falsified outcomes are explicit. Curation loses to templating if F5 − F1 has an interval that includes zero or lies below the seed spread. Curation loses to RAG if F5 − R‑vec does. Organisation erodes if the fixed 60-probe panel's accuracy has a negative slope over replay weeks. Any of these is a result and goes into the thesis as such. E1 already produced a clean negative on C1; this experiment is designed to produce a clean answer either way.

What "best" means is registered before the run. A store is better than another only if its paired accuracy delta at 8 k has a bootstrap interval excluding zero, the delta exceeds the between-seed spread of the headline store, and the difference survives on the sealed confirmation set. Among stores that tie on accuracy, lower search cost per query wins; among those that still tie, lower build cost per thousand events wins. Section 6 gives the exact rule.

2 · Design space

Four axes. Full factorial is 17 stores × 3 harnesses × 3 models × 3 readers × 3 tool sets × 3 budgets, which is thousands of builds. The staged design in section 7 walks the axes one at a time and carries forward only what survived.

Reader tools per family. Fairness means equal budget and equal reader, not identical tools, because a graph cannot be read with cat. File stores get view, regex search, table of contents, and section read. Retrieval stores get one search tool returning ranked chunks. Graph stores get their native query tools. Every tool's return is counted against the same token budget, and the reader stops when the budget is spent.

Periodic designs. F2, F3, and G2 do not fold per window; they consolidate weekly. That is a real design alternative, the "sleep-time" consolidation pattern, and it is registered as such. Their snapshot at T is the last weekly checkpoint, so they carry up to seven days of staleness by construction. The freshness metric records it; the accuracy metric does not forgive it.

Cut order if budget runs out, registered: H1, then G2, then F2, then F4. F0, F1, and all retrieval rows are free and never cut. F5, F6, F7, F3, and G1 are the core of the answer and are cut last.

4 · Yardstick

Unchanged from the E2 plan except for size and the split. The corpus is the R‑H1 slice of the frozen Kafka replay: 37,719 events, January to April warm-up, May to June probe period. Probe families, gold sources, and graders are the E2 ones: extraction, temporal, knowledge update with superseded values, multi-source including code location, abstention. Gold is derived twice, by Python changelog replay and by SQL over the raw JIRA export, and a probe is kept only if both agree.

600 probes, two sealed sets. With seventeen conditions and later a builder grid, picking the best store on the probes it was scored on inflates its accuracy. So two sets of 300, each 60 per family and ≥ 150 entities, are generated and hashed together, and the confirmation set's hash is registered while its content stays unopened until stage F. The corpus supports it: 421 issues touched in the probe period, 187 issues with repeated status transitions, 242 merged PRs carrying an issue key.

Reader agent. One reader for stages B to D: claude-sonnet-5, temperature 0, fixed system prompt, file-native tools, budget on tokens read, 8 k primary with 2 k and 32 k as the accuracy-versus-budget curve. This is a change from the E2 code, where A4 was a deterministic walk. Zhou et al. found reader capability to be the dominant lever on answer quality, so a fixed heuristic reader would blind the experiment to exactly the effect organisation is supposed to have.

Gates before any LLM build, all from the spec and all able to fail: dual-gold agreement ≥ 98 %; retrieve-everything ≥ 0.9 macro; A0 ≤ 0.3 per family on temporal, update, and multi-source; 30-probe two-human pilot with κ ≥ 0.8; leakage gate 100 % on kept probes; equal budget within ±10 % for arms that fill it.

5 · Measurements

Accuracy

correct(p) = 1[norm(answer) = norm(gold)] extraction · temporal · update · abstention
link_F1(p) for multi‑source; file F1 and module hit for code‑location, folded into multi‑source
acc_family(S, B) = mean correct over the family, store S, budget B
macro(S, B) = mean over the five families ← the quantity every contrast is on

Fidelity, the former E2 secondaries

supersession_error(S) = |{p ∈ update : a superseded value appears in the answer}| / |update|
abstention_precision(S) = |{p ∈ abstention : answer = UNKNOWN}| / |abstention|
temporal_acc(S) = acc_family(temporal)
freshness(S) = median(event_time → first snapshot whose diff references the event id)

Cost, beside every accuracy

search_tokens(S, B) = mean tokens read per probe (tool returns + material) ← organisation's payoff in Zhou et al.
tool_calls(S, B) = mean tool rounds per probe
latency(S, B) = median wall seconds per probe
build_cost(S) = Σ(tokens_in + tokens_out) over folds, and $ per 1,000 events at frozen prices
build_wall(S) = hours; failed_folds(S) = rolled‑back or DLQ folds / folds

Store health, at replay weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, and end

files, bytes, files per entity, over_cap = files > 200 lines / files
index_acc = resolving INDEX entries / entries; dangling = unresolved refs / refs
dup_rate = MinHash near‑duplicate fact lines (Jaccard ≥ 0.8) / fact lines
leak_rate = entities with a superseded value still in OVERVIEW / entities with ≥ 1 supersession
B4 = Spearman(tree distance, lexical relatedness) over section pairs ← Zhou et al. taxonomy adherence
scope_leak = share of sections lexically nearer a sibling file than their parent
erosion_slope(S) = OLS slope of the fixed 60‑probe panel's accuracy over replay weeks

Graph stores report the analogues: node and edge counts, edges with closed validity intervals as the supersession measure, orphan nodes as the dangling measure, duplicate entity rate after resolution.

6 · Statistics and decision rule

Every contrast is paired per probe on the identical snapshot, with a BCa bootstrap of 10,000 resamples clustered by entity, and a McNemar test on binary correctness. Entity clusters are primary; probe-family and calendar-week clusters are reported as sensitivity, because the E1 run 4 conclusion moved under a different clustering.

Registered primaries, at 8 k, on the selection set, then the confirmation set

P1 = macro(F5) − macro(F1) curation vs templating
P2 = macro(F5) − macro(R‑vec) curation vs dense RAG
each valid only if |P| > seed_spread(F5) = SD of macro(F5) over seeds 1–3
practical threshold |P| ≥ 0.05, registered

The sweep

All other stores are compared with F5 and with F1 in one Holm-corrected family of contrasts per budget. The output is not one winner but a ranked table with intervals, and the ranking is read with the rule below.

Selection and confirmation

  1. On the selection set, compute macro at 8 k for every store; keep the set of stores whose interval against the top store includes zero. That is the candidate set, not one winner.
  2. Rebuild nothing. Run the candidate stores, plus F1 and R‑vec, on the sealed confirmation set. Report the confirmation numbers as the headline; the selection numbers as a secondary.
  3. Rank candidates by confirmation macro. Ties, meaning intervals that include zero pairwise, are broken by search tokens per query, then by build cost per thousand events.
  4. Stability: the winner must remain in the candidate set under at least two of the three builder cells in stage D and under the Sonnet and Opus readers in stage E. A winner that only wins for one builder or one reader is reported as conditional.

Outcome to implementation, registered now

If the confirmed winner is…Then harnext changes…
F5 or H1Nothing structural. Tune the builder prompt from the health metrics; add embeddings over files if H1 won.
F6 or F7Replace the seeded layout with the winning one; F7 adds the fact ledger and makes supersession a format rule rather than a prompt rule.
F3Batch lane becomes dump‑then‑consolidate: F0 per window, an LLM consolidation pass on a cadence; E5 then sizes the cadence.

8 · Harness, model, reader: what we can and cannot conclude

The interaction you care about, "some harnesses work well with some structures", is stage D. It is a 3 structures × 3 builder cells design on one seed each, plus the Sonnet baseline from stage C, which gives a 3 × 4 table. That detects a large interaction, such as a structure that wins under one builder and loses under another, but not a subtle one; with one seed per cell, a cell difference below the F5 seed spread is noise by our own rule. The honest claims available are "the ranking is stable across builders" or "the ranking flips for structure X under builder Y, and here is the store-shape difference that explains it".

Two harness details matter more than the model name. First, the tool set: Zhou et al. report that changing the tool set alone reshaped the store as strongly as swapping the model, so the shell-only cell is included as a harness variant of the same model. Second, the codex harness does not exist in the builder yet; it is a registry stub. Building it is a code item, and if it slips, the grid degrades to 3 structures × 3 Claude cells and the harness claim is scoped to tool sets.

On the reader side, Zhou et al. found reader strength monotone and builder strength not. If stage E reproduces that on our data, the implementation consequence is that the engine should spend on the reading agent behind the MCP surface rather than on the builder model, which is a cheaper engine to run. That is a thesis-grade finding whichever way it goes.

What a build might cost. The fold count for 37,719 events under the registered windowing is unknown until stage B, but the E1 corpus profile suggests low thousands of folds. At 20 to 60 k input tokens per fold with prompt caching and 1 to 2 k output, one Sonnet build is on the order of a few hundred to two thousand dollars. If the builder runs on Claude Code subscription credentials rather than an API key, the constraint becomes hours rather than dollars, which changes the answer to section 12 question 2 entirely.

Wall time. Builds within one store are serial by design, one writer per org, so a build takes fold count × seconds per fold. Builds across stores are independent and run in parallel on this machine, which has the cores and memory for six at once; they are API-bound, not CPU-bound. Windowing is by event time, so the replay runs as fast as the builder allows rather than at a fixed 60×. A rough shape: if a fold is 45 seconds and there are 6,000 folds, one build is three days, stage C is about a week with six in parallel, and stage D another week. The pilot replaces these guesses.

11 · Risks

  • Build cost is unbounded until the pilot. Mitigation: the pilot is the first paid step of stage C, and the cut order is registered. Nothing in stages A and B depends on it.
  • Winner's curse. Seventeen conditions and a builder grid make the best selection-set number optimistic. Mitigation: the sealed confirmation set and the candidate-set rule; the thesis headline is the confirmation number.
  • Seed spread swallows the effect. The spec's stop rule: if F5's between-seed spread exceeds F5 − F1, the result is "curation does not reliably beat templating at this scale". Mitigation: none, it is a finding; but it is why seeds 2 and 3 for F5 are never cut.
  • Unequal tools across families. A graph reader with a good query tool could win because of the tool, not the store. Mitigation: token budget on tool returns, tool-call counts reported, and the stage E tool-set ladder on file stores shows how much tools alone move accuracy.
  • Snapshot text provenance. As in E1, JIRA text is export-time text. Gold is unaffected; stores are built from it. Declared limitation, informational gate.
  • Periodic designs carry staleness. F2, F3, G2 are up to a week stale at T by construction. Reported as freshness; registered as a property of the design, not a defect of the run.
  • Two E1 patterns to keep watching: a gate that cannot fail, and a human step deferred past the run. Every gate above names a failing value, and the human pilot precedes the first build.

12 · Decisions for you

  1. Scope. Confirm E2 is retired and this plan replaces spec §7 E2 and E3, with the fidelity metrics kept as named secondaries.
  2. Build ceiling and credentials. A dollar ceiling for LLM builds, or a statement that the Claude harness runs on subscription credentials and the ceiling is wall-clock. The pilot will be sized against it.
  3. Condition list. Seventeen as in section 3, or changes. In particular: is F7's fact ledger the structure you want to argue for, and is G1 worth an adapter given the RQ5 recommendation already moved away from a graph as the system of record?
  4. Builder grid. Codex with gpt‑5.x requires building the codex harness. Keep it, or restrict stage D to Claude harness cells (Sonnet, Opus, shell-only) and scope the harness claim to tool sets?
  5. Reader ladder. Haiku, Sonnet, Opus with three tool sets is 45 reader cells and about $450. Keep, or drop Haiku and BM25-augmented cells?
  6. Practical threshold. Register |Δ| ≥ 0.05 macro at 8 k.
  7. Second annotator for the 30-probe pilot, available in stage A.
  8. Keys. Anthropic and Voyage, or OpenAI embeddings in place of Voyage.
